## Summary

If a **neighbour’s fence** sits over what you believe is the boundary line, who helps first is usually a calm written request with your title plan, then a mediation service, then **Citizens Advice** for next steps. A chartered surveyor helps if plans and ground do not match. Advisers will **not** move the fence or start court action for you.

## Key Information

- Download your title plan from HM Land Registry. The red line is often a general boundary, not a precise surveyed line.
- Photograph the posts from both sides with a measuring tape and dated notes.
- Party Wall Act duties apply to some works on shared walls, not every garden fence.
- Escalating with threats usually hardens the dispute. Keep copies of every letter.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

Advisers explain neighbour dispute options and letter structure using [neighbour disputes](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/housing/problems-where-you-live/neighbour-disputes/) guidance. They will not survey the land, force the fence down, or act as your solicitor in court.

## What a surveyor would actually do here

A boundary surveyor can compare title plans, historic deeds, and physical features, then give a professional opinion. They will not mediate the relationship, or guarantee the neighbour accepts their plan without further process.

## Practical Guidance

- Get the **title plan** and any deed plans that mention fences or hedges.
- Speak once if safe, then write a short letter with photos and a proposed line.
- Try a community **mediation** service before solicitors.
- Ask **Citizens Advice** if letters escalate or you face court threats.
- Instruct a **surveyor** if the paperwork and ground still conflict.

## FAQ

**Can the council force them to move it?**
Councils rarely police private boundary lines. Planning enforcement is a different issue if they needed permission for height or location.

**What if they claim adverse possession?**
That is a specialised Land Registry process. Get advice before you concede anything in writing.
