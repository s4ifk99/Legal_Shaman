## Summary

If a neighbour in a private **HMO** **keeps you awake** at night, who helps is the landlord or agent first, then the council noise or environmental health team. The police are for a crime or an immediate risk, not for every loud evening. **Citizens Advice** can explain a complaint letter. They will not stay overnight, mediate the argument, or rehouse you the same night.

## Key Information

- Keep a simple log: date, time, what you heard, and how long it went on. A single loud night is a weaker report than a pattern.
- The landlord or agent is the first door because the tenancy and house rules sit with them. Ask what they will do and by when.
- The council noise team is the next door when the landlord does not stop a pattern.
- Call the police when there is violence, threats, or a crime in progress. They will not act as the noise complaint service for ordinary loud music.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Under the **Environmental Protection Act 1990** the council must look into complaints of statutory nuisance, which can include loud noise at night. If it agrees there is a nuisance it can serve an abatement notice.
- Most **HMOs** must meet management rules under the **Housing Act 2004**, and larger ones need a council licence. The landlord or manager is responsible for running the property.
- Persistent anti social behaviour can be raised with the council and police. The **Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014** also lets you ask for a case review (the community trigger) if repeated reports get no response.
- A noise log (dates, times, what you heard) is the evidence councils usually ask for.

## Practical Guidance

- Start a log of nights: date, time, and how long it went on.
- Write to the landlord or agent with the log. Ask for a written reply.
- If the night noise continues, report it to the council noise or environmental health team.
- Use the Citizens Advice noisy neighbours guide before you write a second letter.
- Call the police only if there is a crime or you are at risk.
