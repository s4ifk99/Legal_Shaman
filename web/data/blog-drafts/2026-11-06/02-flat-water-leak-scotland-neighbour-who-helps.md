## Summary

After a **water leak** from your flat in Scotland, who helps is usually your factor or landlord, both insurers, then **Citizens Advice Scotland** for how to answer detail requests without guessing **liability**. Advisers explain negligence versus accident language and point to letter templates. They will **not** decide who pays, chase your neighbour, or represent you in court.

## Key Information

- Liability is not automatic because water came from your flat. Advisers look at carelessness versus a sudden unforeseeable failure.
- Keep photos, plumber reports, stopcock times, and any messages. Do not invent causes.
- If you rent, give the neighbour the landlord or factor contact rather than personal finances.
- Buildings insurance often leads on structural damage. Contents insurance covers belongings.
- Sibling wiki: England neighbour leak pages still help for evidence habits, but use Scotland doors for process.

## What Citizens Advice Scotland would actually do here

Advisers explain how accidental damage claims and negligence claims differ on their Scotland pages. They point you to [disputes about damages and repairs](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/housing/neighbour-disputes-s/disputes-about-damages-and-repairs/) and [water leaks and pipes](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/consumer/water/water-leaks-and-pipes-s/). They help you draft a short factual reply: what you know, what you are fixing, insurer details if appropriate. They will not decide liability, speak to the neighbour for you, or lodge a court claim.

## What your insurer would actually do here

They open an escape of water or similar claim if cover applies, and may contact the neighbour’s insurer about recovery later. They will not always admit you are at fault just because the leak started upstairs.

## Practical Guidance

- Make the property safe: stopcock, plumber, photos.
- **Factor / landlord / agent** if flats share pipes or you rent.
- **Your insurer** (and ask the neighbour to use theirs).
- Reply to detail requests with facts only: dates, works done, contacts. Avoid blame fights in chat apps.
- **Citizens Advice Scotland** if the neighbour demands money or personal data you should not share.
- Mediation or a solicitor only if a formal claim starts and free doors are exhausted.

## FAQ

**Must I hand over bank details or full insurance documents?**
Share what your insurer and factor say is needed. Do not send passwords or unrelated personal files.

**Neighbour says you are automatically liable.**
That is a claim, not a final decision. Use insurer and Citizens Advice Scotland before you pay cash.
