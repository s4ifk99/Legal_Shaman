## Summary

If a **neighbour** is **blocking** your **shared driveway**, the council and the police usually cannot move the car, because it is private land. Who helps depends on the documents: a right of way in the title, a space in the lease, or estate rules. Start with those documents, then the managing agent if there is one. **Citizens Advice** can explain a neighbour complaint letter. They will not tow the car or decide the boundary.

## Key Information

- Do not clamp, block in, or damage the other car. Clamping on private land is restricted. The allocated space page points at the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012, section 54.
- A **shared driveway** is not the same as an allocated parking space. If the problem is your numbered bay, use the allocated space page. Use this page when the shared access itself is blocked.
- The existing shared driveway pages explain common rights. They are not a who helps path. Link them. Do not copy them into a second explainer.
- Check the title or lease before you accuse the neighbour. [GOV.UK: get information about property and land](https://www.gov.uk/get-information-about-property-and-land).
- If you own a leasehold flat, the managing agent is often the door that can enforce estate rules. [LEASE](https://www.lease-advice.org/) explains leasehold. They will not come and move the car.

## What the managing agent would actually do here

They can enforce lease and estate rules where the documents give them that power, and they can write to the resident. They will not always attend the same day, and they cannot invent a right that the lease does not give.

## What the council and police would actually do here

On private land they usually will not ticket or tow a neighbour's car that is **blocking** a **shared driveway**. Call the police if there is a threat or a crime, not because the car is in the way.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

Advisers explain **problems with neighbours** and how to complain in writing. They will not mediate on the drive or remove the vehicle. Guide: [problems with neighbours](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/housing/problems-with-neighbours/).

## Practical Guidance

- Confirm whether this is a **shared driveway** or an allocated bay. Use the allocated space page for a numbered bay.
- Read the title, the lease, or the estate rules. Use the GOV.UK land information service if you need the title.
- Write to the neighbour, or to the managing agent if the rules sit with them. Ask them to keep the drive clear. Keep a copy.
- Do not clamp the car.
- Use the Citizens Advice neighbour guide if you need the shape of a complaint letter.
- If there is a threat, contact the police. A blocked drive on its own is not that call.
- Link the shared driveway explainers for the rights background, and this page for who to contact.

## FAQ

**Can I block their car in until they move?**

No. Do not obstruct or clamp them. Write, then use the managing agent if you have one.

**Is the council the first call?**

Not for a private shared drive. Check the documents and the managing agent first.
