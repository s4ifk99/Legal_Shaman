## Summary

For a **private parking charge** on a residential estate, who helps is the parking operator’s appeal process first, then **POPLA** or **IAS** if you get a rejection code, plus your landlord, freeholder, or managing agent if the bay is yours under a lease. **Citizens Advice** helps you sort contract versus visitor bay rules. Advisers will **not** cancel the charge for you.

## Key Information

- Residential estate tickets are usually private land charges, not council PCNs.
- Photograph signs at entry and at the bay, the bay markings, and your lease or AST clauses about parking.
- Residents sometimes win on inadequate signs, wrong VRM, or no keeper liability paperwork. Deadlines still matter.
- If you are a visitor, ask the resident for written confirmation of the bay you used.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

Advisers explain [parking tickets](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/cars/parking-tickets/) paths and how private appeals differ from council ones. They will not lodge POPLA for you, rewrite your lease, or attend the hearing as your advocate.

## What POPLA or IAS would actually do here

They decide after the operator rejects your first appeal, if the operator is in that scheme and you have the code. They will not settle a lease dispute with the freeholder, or hear a council PCN.

## Practical Guidance

- Appeal to the **operator** within the notice deadline with photos and lease excerpts.
- If rejected, use **POPLA** (BPA) or **IAS** (IPC) with the verification code.
- Write to the **managing agent** or freeholder if signs or permits changed without clear notice.
- Ask **Citizens Advice** before you ignore debt collector letters.

## FAQ

**It is my allocated bay.**
Put the lease plan and bay number in the operator appeal. Still meet the appeal deadline.

**Is this the same as a council PCN?**
Usually no. Look for “Parking Charge” and a private company logo.
