## Summary

If a finance company has **taken back** a car on **conditional sale** or hire purchase, check the credit agreement before you pay any further sum. The lender is not the same as the dealer. **Citizens Advice** explains when goods can be taken back and how to end the agreement in writing. A free debt adviser can look at what is left to pay. Neither will negotiate the debt for you or represent you in court.

## Key Information

- The goods stay with the lender until the agreement is paid. Do not sell the car to raise the money.
- **Taken back** is not the same as you choosing to end the agreement. Ending it is done in writing. The Citizens Advice letter is the starting point, not a phone promise from the dealer.
- The hire purchase guide explains the difference between you ending the agreement and the lender ending it, including when a court order is part of taking the goods.
- Check the credit agreement for the total price and what it says you still owe if the agreement ends. If the lender demands the full remaining balance just to let you end it, the Citizens Advice page says that demand is wrong and to speak to an adviser.
- If the car is sold after it is **taken back** and a balance remains, ask the lender for the sale figure in writing before you agree a plan.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

Advisers will explain hire purchase and **conditional sale**, point you at the end agreement letter, and help you see whether the lender or you ended the agreement. They will not collect the car, bid at auction, or run the court case. Use the guide first, then the letter: [Hire purchase and conditional sale](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/debt-and-money/borrowing-money/types-of-borrowing/hire-purchase-and-conditional-sale/) and [End a hire purchase or a conditional sale agreement](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/debt-and-money/template-letters-to-creditors/end-a-hire-purchase-or-a-conditional-sale-agreement/).

## What a free debt adviser would actually do here

National Debtline and StepChange will help you list what is owed and what you can pay. They will not tell the lender to give the car back, and they will not guarantee the balance disappears because the car was sold. [National Debtline](https://www.nationaldebtline.org/) and [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org/).

## Practical Guidance

- Find the credit agreement and any notice that the car was **taken back**.
- Read the Citizens Advice hire purchase guide before you agree a lump sum.
- If you want to end the agreement yourself, use the Citizens Advice letter. Do not rely on the dealer to end it.
- Ask the lender, in writing, for the sale price if they already sold the car and for the balance they say is left.
- Speak to National Debtline or StepChange if a balance remains and you cannot pay it.
- Read the related wiki pages on hire purchase and on ending the agreement. This page is the situation. Those pages are the definition and the letter.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as what is conditional sale?**

No. That definition query already has a page, and it sits well back in Google. This page is for the moment the car is **taken back**.

**Can the dealer sort this?**

The dealer sold the car. The finance company is usually the other party to the agreement. Write to the lender named on the agreement.

**Should I pay the full balance today to get the car back?**

Do not agree a figure you have not checked against the agreement and the Citizens Advice guide.
