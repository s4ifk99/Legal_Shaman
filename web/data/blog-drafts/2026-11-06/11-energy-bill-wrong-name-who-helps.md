## Summary

If an **energy bill** arrives in the **wrong name**, dispute it in writing with the supplier. Say who lives at the address and from when. **Citizens Advice** explains energy bill problems and the complaint path. The **Energy Ombudsman** can look at the complaint after eight weeks or a final response. They will not correct the account on the day you phone, and Citizens Advice will not phone the supplier for you.

## Key Information

- This is not the page for a debt at an address you never lived at. Use that page if you have never lived there. Use this page if the name on a bill for your home is wrong.
- Put the dispute in writing. Keep the bill, the account number, and proof of who lives there, such as a tenancy start date. Do not send documents you do not need to share.
- Ask the supplier to correct the name and to pause chasing while they check the dispute.
- Complain to the supplier first. The Energy Ombudsman is the next door after eight weeks or a final response, not the first call.
- If a debt collector is already chasing a bill in someone else's name, say it is disputed and point them back to the supplier.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Responsibility for an **energy bill** usually follows the person who lives at the property and uses the supply, whether under a contract or a deemed contract that starts when you move in.
- Suppliers are regulated by Ofgem and must handle complaints fairly and correct account errors such as a **wrong name**.
- If the supplier has not resolved the complaint within eight weeks, or sends a deadlock letter, the Energy Ombudsman can look at it for free.
- Keep proof of your move in date, meter readings and tenancy details. They show when your responsibility started.

## Practical Guidance

- Check the name and address on the bill against who actually lives there.
- Write to the supplier. Say the name is wrong, ask them to correct it, and ask them not to chase while they check.
- Read the Citizens Advice page on energy bill problems before you pay a sum you dispute.
- If a collector writes, tell them the debt is disputed and send them back to the supplier.
- If the supplier does not fix it in eight weeks, or sends a final response you reject, go to the Energy Ombudsman.
- If you never lived at the address, use the related page instead of this one.
