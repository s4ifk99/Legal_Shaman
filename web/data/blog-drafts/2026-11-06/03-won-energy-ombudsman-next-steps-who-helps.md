## Summary

If you already won at the **Energy Ombudsman**, who helps next is the Ombudsman compliance team if the supplier misses the award deadline, then **Citizens Advice** consumer service for wider patterns, and **Ofgem** for licence breach reports. They will **not** reinvestigate the same dispute as a fresh case just because you want a fraud label.

## Key Information

- Keep the Ombudsman decision letter and any payment or account correction deadlines.
- If the supplier misses the deadline, go back to the Ombudsman first: that is the enforcement lever for your award.
- “Fraud” is a high bar. Most energy billing disputes stay in ombudsman and regulator channels, not police.
- A win does not automatically trigger a public fine. Regulators decide whether to open a separate case.

## What the Energy Ombudsman would actually do here

They issue a binding decision the supplier should follow, and chase compliance if you report non payment or incomplete remedy within their process. They will not open a criminal fraud investigation, fine the supplier for the market, or give you a second award on the same facts.

## Practical Guidance

- **Energy Ombudsman**: confirm the award is paid or implemented; escalate non compliance.
- **Citizens Advice consumer service**: report ongoing poor practice for intelligence (they may pass patterns to Trading Standards / Ofgem pathways).
- **Ofgem**: use their contact/report channels for licence concerns after you have the Ombudsman outcome.

## FAQ

**Should I report the supplier to the police for fraud?**
Usually no for a billing dispute you already won. Use Ombudsman compliance and Ofgem/Citizens Advice reporting first.

**What if they still have not paid?**
Tell the Ombudsman immediately with dates. That is the first lever for your award.
