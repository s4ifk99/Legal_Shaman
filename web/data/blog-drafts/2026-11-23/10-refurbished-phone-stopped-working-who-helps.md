## Summary

If a **refurbished phone stopped working**, you still have rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 when you bought it from a business. Refurbished goods must be of satisfactory quality for their age and price. Who helps: ask the seller for a repair or replacement, then a refund, then your card issuer, then the Financial Ombudsman if the bank says no. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** chase the seller for you.

## Key Information

- Within 30 days of delivery you can reject a faulty item for a full refund.
- After 30 days and within six months, the seller gets one chance to repair or replace. If that fails, you can ask for a refund. The fault is assumed to have been there at delivery unless the seller proves otherwise.
- Check who the seller is on a marketplace. Your rights are against the seller, but marketplaces often have their own guarantees.
- Buying from a private individual gives fewer rights than buying from a business.
- If you paid by card, a **chargeback** may help. **Section 75** applies to credit card purchases between £100 and £30,000.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Refurbished goods bought from a business must be of satisfactory quality, as described and fit for purpose under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015**. Satisfactory quality takes account of age and price.
- Within 30 days of delivery you can reject a faulty item for a full refund.
- After that, the seller gets one chance to repair or replace. If that fails, you can ask for a price reduction or a refund.
- A fault that appears within six months is assumed to have been there at delivery unless the seller proves otherwise.

## Practical Guidance

- Write to the **seller** describing the fault and asking for a repair or replacement, quoting the **Consumer Rights Act 2015**.
- If that fails, ask for a **refund**, and use the marketplace guarantee if there is one.
- Ask your card issuer about **chargeback** or **Section 75**. If the bank refuses, complain and then go to the **Financial Ombudsman Service**.
- **Financial Ombudsman Service**: [Open](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/).
- **Citizens Advice Adviceline (England)**: [Open](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/contact-us/contact-us/advice-line/).

## FAQ

**Are refurbished phones covered by the Consumer Rights Act?**

Yes, if you bought from a business. They must be of satisfactory quality, taking into account that they are refurbished and the price you paid.

**It has been six weeks. Can I still get a refund?**

The 30 day right to reject has passed, so the seller can try a repair or replacement first. If that fails, you can ask for a refund.

**The seller vanished. What now?**

Contact your card issuer about chargeback or Section 75, and report the seller to the marketplace.
