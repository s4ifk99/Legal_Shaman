## Summary

If you got a **parking charge** because the **pay machine was broken**, the new Private Parking Code of Practice helps you. It came into force on 11 November 2026 and its Appeals Charter says a private charge should be cancelled where you show the machines were out of order and there was no reasonable alternative way to pay. Who helps: appeal to the operator first, then POPLA or the IAS. Council tickets go to a tribunal instead. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** appeal for you.

## Key Information

- Check who issued it. A **private parking charge** (from a company) and a council **Penalty Charge Notice** follow different appeal routes.
- Take photos of the broken machine, any out of order notice, and the signs about other ways to pay.
- Save evidence you tried: a failed card payment, a call to the payment line, or an app error screenshot.
- Under the new code, a working nearby machine or a phone or app option may count as a reasonable alternative. Say why it was not available to you.
- The new code is not retrospective. Charges from before 11 November 2026 follow the old rules, but a broken machine is still a strong appeal point.

## Practical Guidance

- Appeal to the **parking operator** in writing with your photos and payment attempts, quoting the **Appeals Charter**.
- If they reject it, check the rejection letter: use **POPLA** for BPA members or the **IAS** for IPC members.
- For a council ticket, make informal and formal representations to the council, then go to the tribunal.

## FAQ

**Will my parking charge be cancelled if the pay machine was broken?**

Under the new code it should be, if you show the machine was out of order and there was no reasonable alternative to pay. Evidence makes the difference.

**The app was available but had no signal. Does that count?**

Possibly. Explain why the alternative was not reasonable for you, with screenshots or notes.

**Is this a fine?**

A private **parking charge** is a contractual charge, not a criminal fine. A council **Penalty Charge Notice** is different and goes to a tribunal on appeal.
