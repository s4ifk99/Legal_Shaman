## Summary

If you got a **parking charge** because the **pay machine was broken**, you can appeal. The government laid a new Private Parking Code of Practice in Parliament as a draft on 20 October 2026. It is not in force yet: it can only be issued after 40 days of Parliamentary scrutiny and then starts 21 days later. Once in force, its Appeals Charter says a private charge should be cancelled where you show the machines were out of order and there was no reasonable alternative way to pay. Who helps: appeal to the operator first, then POPLA or the IAS. Council tickets go to a tribunal instead. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** appeal for you.

## Key Information

- Check who issued it. A **private parking charge** (from a company) and a council **Penalty Charge Notice** follow different appeal routes.
- Take photos of the broken machine, any out of order notice, and the signs about other ways to pay.
- Save evidence you tried: a failed card payment, a call to the payment line, or an app error screenshot.
- Under the draft code, a working nearby machine or a phone or app option may count as a reasonable alternative. Say why it was not available to you.
- Until the new code is in force, operators follow their trade body's code (BPA or IPC). A broken machine with evidence that you tried to pay is still a strong appeal point.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- A private **parking charge** is a contractual charge, not a fine. Keeper liability rules are in Schedule 4 of the **Protection of Freedoms Act 2012**.
- The new **Private Parking Code of Practice**, made under the **Parking (Code of Practice) Act 2019**, was laid in Parliament as a draft on 20 October 2026. It includes an Appeals Charter saying a charge should be cancelled on appeal where payment machines were out of order and there was no reasonable alternative way to pay.
- The Code is not yet in force. GOV.UK says it can be issued after 40 days of Parliamentary scrutiny and comes into force 21 days after it is issued. Check GOV.UK for the confirmed start date.
- Council Penalty Charge Notices are different. They follow the **Traffic Management Act 2004** and go to an independent tribunal on appeal.

## Practical Guidance

- Appeal to the **parking operator** in writing with your photos and payment attempts, quoting the **Appeals Charter**.
- If they reject it, check the rejection letter: use **POPLA** for BPA members or the **IAS** for IPC members.
- For a council ticket, make informal and formal representations to the council, then go to the tribunal.

## FAQ

**Will my parking charge be cancelled if the pay machine was broken?**

Once the new code is in force it should be, if you show the machine was out of order and there was no reasonable alternative to pay. Until then, it is still a strong appeal point. Evidence makes the difference.

**The app was available but had no signal. Does that count?**

Possibly. Explain why the alternative was not reasonable for you, with screenshots or notes.

**Is this a fine?**

A private **parking charge** is a contractual charge, not a criminal fine. A council **Penalty Charge Notice** is different and goes to a tribunal on appeal.
