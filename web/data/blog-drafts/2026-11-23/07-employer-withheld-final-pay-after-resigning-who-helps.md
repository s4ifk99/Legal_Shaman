## Summary

If your **employer withheld your final pay** after you resigned, it may be an unlawful deduction from wages. **Unpaid final wages** usually include hours worked up to your last day and any unused holiday you built up. Who helps: a written chase to payroll, then ACAS early conciliation, then the free employment doors Matching Help returns. Those doors will **not** recover the money as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Your final pay should include all hours or days worked, any notice pay owed, and pay for holiday you built up but did not take.
- An employer can only deduct money if your contract allows it in writing or you agreed in advance. Ask which clause they rely on.
- Check your last payslip and compare it with your timesheets.
- Before an employment tribunal claim you must contact ACAS for early conciliation. The time limit is short, usually three months less one day from the date you should have been paid.
- You can also claim in the small claims court for breach of contract in some cases. Ask a free adviser which route fits.

## Practical Guidance

- Email **payroll or HR** with the dates and amounts you are owed and a deadline to pay.
- Ask for the contract clause if they say a **deduction** is allowed.
- Start **ACAS early conciliation** if it is still unpaid, before the time limit.

## FAQ

**Can my employer keep my final pay because I did not work my full notice?**

They may be able to deduct for notice you did not work only if the contract allows it. They cannot keep pay for hours you already worked without a lawful reason.

**Do I get paid for unused holiday when I leave?**

Yes. Holiday you built up but did not take should be paid in your **final pay**.

**What if they ignore me?**

Start ACAS early conciliation. If that does not settle it, you can make a tribunal claim for **unpaid wages**.
