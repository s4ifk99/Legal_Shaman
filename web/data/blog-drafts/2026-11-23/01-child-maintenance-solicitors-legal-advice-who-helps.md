## Summary

Most people looking for **child maintenance solicitors** do not need one to start. **Legal advice on child maintenance** usually begins with free, impartial help from Child Maintenance Options, then either a family based arrangement or the Child Maintenance Service (CMS). A solicitor matters more when the case goes beyond the CMS: very high incomes, school fees, a child with a disability, or money tied up in a divorce. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** collect money for you or represent you in court.

## Key Information

- **Child maintenance** is usually worked out by the CMS formula, or agreed privately between parents as a family based arrangement.
- You normally have to contact Child Maintenance Options before you apply to the CMS. They explain both routes for free.
- The CMS can calculate, collect and enforce payments. Courts usually only deal with **child maintenance** in limited cases, such as top ups above the CMS limit, school fees, or a consent order on divorce.
- Keep a record of every payment received or missed, with dates and amounts. You will need it whichever route you pick.
- If the CMS gets something wrong, there is a complaints route and then the Independent Case Examiner. You do not need a solicitor for that.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- In England and Wales most **child maintenance** is handled by the Child Maintenance Service (CMS) under the **Child Support Act 1991**, or by a family based arrangement between parents.
- Courts can only decide child maintenance in limited cases, for example a consent order, top up payments above the CMS maximum, school fees or costs linked to disability.
- The CMS works out maintenance using a set formula based on the paying parent's income and how many nights the child stays with them.
- **Child maintenance solicitors** are mostly needed for court based issues or when maintenance is part of a divorce settlement. Family legal aid is limited and usually needs evidence of domestic abuse or child abuse.

## Practical Guidance

- Call **Child Maintenance Options** first for free, impartial information on a family based arrangement versus the CMS.
- If the other parent will not agree or pay, apply to the **Child Maintenance Service** and keep your case reference.
- Use the CMS complaints process if a calculation or enforcement step looks wrong, before going further.
- Speak to **child maintenance solicitors** if the case involves high income, school fees, disability costs or a divorce financial order.

## FAQ

**Do I need child maintenance solicitors to get maintenance paid?**

Usually not. Most parents use a family based arrangement or the CMS without a solicitor. **Legal advice on child maintenance** from a solicitor helps most where the court, not the CMS, has the power to decide.

**Can I get free legal advice on child maintenance?**

Child Maintenance Options gives free information. The free doors below may give advice or point you to local help. Legal aid for **child maintenance** on its own is limited, so check eligibility with the Legal Aid Agency link before you rely on it.

**The CMS is not enforcing my case. What next?**

Complain to the CMS in writing first. If that does not fix it, the Independent Case Examiner can look at how the CMS handled your case.
