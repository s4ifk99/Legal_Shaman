## Summary

If you are **applying for sole custody in the UK**, the court in England and Wales calls it a **child arrangements order** saying who the child lives with. **Sole custody** is not a legal term here, and the court decides based on the child's welfare. Who helps starts with mediation (usually a MIAM first), free family law advice lines, and a legal aid check if there has been domestic abuse. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** apply to court for you.

## Key Information

- In England and Wales, **sole custody** is decided through a **child arrangements order** (who the child lives with and spends time with).
- Before applying you normally need a Mediation Information and Assessment Meeting (MIAM). There are exemptions, for example where there is evidence of domestic abuse or urgency.
- The application is made on form C100 to the family court, online or on paper. There is a court fee unless you get help with fees.
- Cafcass usually carries out safety checks and may write a report for the court.
- Legal aid is mostly limited to cases with evidence of domestic abuse or child protection concerns, but mediation can be covered if you qualify financially.
- Scotland and Northern Ireland have different systems. This page is about England and Wales.

## Practical Guidance

- Write down the current arrangements, any safety concerns, and what you think is best for the child.
- Book a **MIAM** with an accredited family mediator, or check whether an exemption applies to you.
- If mediation fails or is not safe, apply on **form C100** for a **child arrangements order**.
- Check legal aid eligibility early, especially where there is domestic abuse.

## FAQ

**Can I get sole custody in the UK?**

The court can order that a child lives with one parent, and can limit or stop contact where that is in the child's interest. When **applying for sole custody in the UK**, the court focuses on the child's welfare, not on which parent wins.

**Do I need a solicitor to apply?**

No. Many parents apply for a **child arrangements order** without a lawyer. Free family advice lines can explain the forms and the process, and a solicitor helps most in contested or safety cases.

**Do I have to go to mediation first?**

Usually you must attend a MIAM before applying. Exemptions exist, including some domestic abuse and urgent situations.
