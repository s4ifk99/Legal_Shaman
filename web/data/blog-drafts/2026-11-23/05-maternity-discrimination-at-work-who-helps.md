## Summary

**Maternity discrimination at work** means being treated unfavourably because you are pregnant, on maternity leave, or for a related illness. It is unlawful under the Equality Act 2010. Who helps starts with writing down what happened and raising a grievance, then ACAS early conciliation, then the free employment doors Matching Help returns. Time limits are short, so do not wait. Those doors will **not** run the tribunal claim as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- You are protected whether you are an employee or a worker, and from the start of the job. Many rights apply from day one.
- Common examples: being dismissed or chosen for redundancy, losing shifts or a promotion, or being pushed out after you told your employer you were pregnant.
- Keep a timeline with dates, messages, and who said what. Note when you told your employer about the pregnancy.
- Before most tribunal claims you must contact ACAS for early conciliation. Tribunal time limits are short, usually three months less one day from the act. Check ACAS for the current rule.
- Speak to a solicitor if you have been dismissed, the deadline is close, or ACAS conciliation has ended.

## FAQ

**What counts as maternity discrimination at work?**

Unfavourable treatment because of pregnancy, maternity leave, or a pregnancy related illness. Dismissal or redundancy selection for these reasons is one of the most common cases.

**How long do I have to act?**

Usually three months less one day from the act to start ACAS early conciliation. The rules on time limits are changing, so check ACAS straight away.

**Do I need a solicitor?**

Not to start. The free doors below can explain your rights. A solicitor is worth it for dismissal, large losses, or when the deadline is close.
