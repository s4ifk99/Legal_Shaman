## Summary

If a **private dentist** issued a surprise bill after treatment, who helps starts with the written quote and a complaint to the practice, then the Dental Complaints Service where private care fits, then the free doors Matching Help returns. Those free doors will **not** rewrite the invoice for you or sue the dentist as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Keep the treatment plan, any quote you signed, and the final invoice.
- Ask which items were extras versus what you already agreed in writing.
- Private dental complaints often go to the Dental Complaints Service before court.
- NHS and private mixed care can split which complaint route applies. Note which teeth were private.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Private dental treatment is a service under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015**. If a price was agreed or given in writing, that price normally binds the practice.
- If no price was agreed, you only have to pay a reasonable price.
- The General Dental Council expects dentists to give clear treatment plans and cost estimates before treatment.
- If the practice does not resolve the complaint, the Dental Complaints Service handles complaints about private dental care for free.

## FAQ

**They say I verbally agreed to extras in the chair.**

Ask for that agreement in writing and for a line by line explanation. Without a clear quote for the extras, keep disputing the surprise amounts.
