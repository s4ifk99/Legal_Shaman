## Summary

If a **dry cleaner lost or ruined** your clothes, who helps starts with a written complaint and proof of value to the cleaner, then card reclaim where you paid by card, then the free doors Matching Help returns. Those free doors will **not** replace the garments for you or sue the cleaner as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Keep the ticket, photos of the damage, and proof of what you paid for the clothes.
- Many cleaners try to cap liability in small print. That cap is not always the last word.
- Ask for repair, replacement cost, or a fair cash sum in one clear letter.
- Trading Standards report paths can run in parallel with your money claim prep.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Dry cleaning is a service. Under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015** the cleaner must use reasonable care and skill.
- If the **dry cleaner lost or ruined** an item through a lack of care, you can ask for the work to be redone or for compensation, usually based on the item's value at the time, not its new price.
- A ticket or sign limiting liability cannot remove the duty of reasonable care.
- If the cleaner refuses, the small claims track of the county court is the usual next step.

## Practical Guidance

- Complain in writing to the **dry cleaner** with the ticket number, photos, and what you want.
- If you paid by **card**, ask the issuer about chargeback once the cleaner rejects a fair offer.

## FAQ

**They say the ticket limits them to a few pounds.**

Ask them to show the clause you agreed. You can still propose a fairer figure based on proof of value and keep escalating if they refuse.
