## Summary

If a **delivery** was left with a neighbour and then went missing, who helps starts with the seller and courier complaint trails, then your card issuer where you paid by card, then the free doors Matching Help returns. Those free doors will **not** retrieve the parcel for you or sue the courier as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Keep tracking screenshots, delivery photo, and neighbour name if known.
- Contract is usually with the seller, not only the courier.
- Safe place instructions you never agreed can matter for liability arguments.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015** goods bought from a business stay at the seller's risk until they reach you or someone you nominated to receive them.
- If you did not nominate the neighbour, a **delivery left with a neighbour** that goes missing is usually still the seller's problem, not yours. The claim is against the seller, not the courier.
- If you did ask for it to go to that neighbour, the risk usually passed when they took it.
- If the seller refuses, a card **chargeback** or **Section 75** of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 (credit card purchases over £100 and up to £30,000) may help.
