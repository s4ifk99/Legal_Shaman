## Summary

If a **mobile** network quotes an early exit fee you think is wrong, who helps starts with the network complaint, then Alternative Dispute Resolution where the network is signed up, then the free doors Matching Help returns. Those free doors will **not** cancel the contract for you or sue the network as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Keep the contract start date, notice period, and the written exit fee quote.
- Ask for a full breakdown of handset subsidy versus airtime.
- Ofcom rules and ADR schemes cover many UK mobile providers.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Mobile contracts are regulated by Ofcom. Providers must tell you any price rises in pounds and pence at the point of sale.
- If a provider changes the contract in a way that is not to your benefit and was not set out when you signed, Ofcom rules usually let you leave without an **early exit fee**.
- Outside those cases, an **early exit fee** set out in the contract is normally payable, but it must be fair under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015**.
- If the complaint is not resolved after eight weeks, or you get a deadlock letter, you can take it to the provider's ADR scheme (Communications Ombudsman or CISAS) for free.
