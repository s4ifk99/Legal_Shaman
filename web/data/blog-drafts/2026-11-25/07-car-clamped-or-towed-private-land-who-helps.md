## Summary

If your car was **clamped or towed** on private land in England and Wales, that is often unlawful. The Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 makes it an offence for a private operator to immobilise a vehicle without lawful authority, and it also restricts private towing. Official bodies such as the police or a council may still have lawful authority in some cases. Who helps: the police if the car is being held unlawfully, the council if it was a council removal, and advice services plus the parking operator's appeals process if you were given a private parking charge instead. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** collect the car for you.

## Key Information

- Private clamping on private land is generally banned in England and Wales. A private firm should not be demanding a release fee to take a clamp off.
- Private towing on private land is also tightly restricted. A ticket or a private parking charge is the usual path, not removal of the car.
- If the police, a council, or another body with lawful authority removed the car (for example from a public road, or as an abandoned vehicle), different rules apply. Use the notice you were given and the council or police recovery scheme.
- Photograph the scene, the signs, and any paperwork. Note the time, the location, and any company name or phone number.
- If you have a private parking charge rather than a clamp, use the operator's appeal process and, if needed, the independent appeals service named on the notice.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Under the **Protection of Freedoms Act 2012**, it is an offence in England and Wales to immobilise a vehicle on private land without lawful authority. Private clamping for a parking charge is not lawful authority.
- The same Act restricts moving a vehicle on private land without lawful authority. Private car parks usually rely on a parking contract and a charge, not on towing.
- The police and local authorities can still have powers to remove vehicles in the situations set out in law, for example on a public road or where a vehicle appears abandoned.
- Paying a release fee under protest does not always end the matter. Keep evidence and get advice before you pay if you can do so safely.

## Practical Guidance

- Work out who removed or clamped the car: a private firm, the council, or the police. The notice or signs should say.
- If a private firm has clamped the car, or is holding it after a private tow, contact the **police** (101, or 999 if you are unsafe) and record the company name.
- If it is a council or police removal, follow the official recovery instructions and ask about the legal basis and the fees.
- If you were given a private parking charge only, appeal in time and keep copies.

## FAQ

**Is private clamping legal in England and Wales?**

In almost all private parking situations, no. There are narrow exceptions for people with lawful authority, not for a private parking company collecting a charge.

**The operator will not tell me where my car is.**

If a private firm has taken it, contact the police and keep a record. If a council or the police removed it, use their official vehicle recovery service.

**I already paid a release fee. Can I still get help?**

Keep the receipt and photos, and get advice about recovering the money. Do not delay, because time limits can be short.
