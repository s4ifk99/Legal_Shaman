## Summary

If a **Black Friday advertised discount** was not a genuine reduction, that can be a misleading price practice. Traders must not use a "was" price or a claimed percentage off in a way that deceives shoppers. Who helps: complain to the trader first, then the Citizens Advice consumer service, which can pass a report to Trading Standards, and the Competition and Markets Authority for widespread practices. If you already bought the item, your usual refund and card protection routes may also apply. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** take the trader to court for you.

## Key Information

- Keep screenshots of the advertised price, the "was" price, the date, and the checkout total. Save the confirmation email.
- Write to the trader, point out the advertised discount, and ask for a refund of the difference or to cancel if you would not have bought at the true price.
- A genuine reference price should reflect a real, recent selling price, not a figure that was only shown for a short time to make the discount look larger.
- You can report misleading pricing to the Citizens Advice consumer service, which passes cases to Trading Standards. You can also report a widespread practice to the Competition and Markets Authority.
- If you paid by card, ask about chargeback or **Section 75** where the facts support a claim.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- The **Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024** sets the current rules on unfair commercial practices, including misleading pricing. These rules replaced the earlier Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations for practices from April 2025.
- A trader must not mislead you about the price or about a claimed discount. A "was" price should be a genuine reference price.
- Trading Standards enforce many of these rules, often after a report through Citizens Advice. The Competition and Markets Authority can act on widespread or national practices.
- Buying the item does not stop you complaining. You may also have contract rights or card protection if the price claim was part of why you bought.

## Practical Guidance

- Save screenshots and the order confirmation, then complain to the **trader** in writing and ask for the difference or a cancellation.
- Report misleading pricing to the **Citizens Advice consumer service** so that Trading Standards can consider it.
- For a widespread practice, you can also report it to the **Competition and Markets Authority**.
- If you paid by card and you want your money back, contact your **card issuer** as well.

## FAQ

**The item was only at the higher "was" price for a few days. Does that matter?**

It can. A reference price should be genuine. A short, inflated "was" price used to make a discount look larger can be misleading. Report it and include your screenshots.

**I already bought the item. Can I still get money back?**

You can complain to the trader and ask for the difference or a refund. Card protection may also be available. A Trading Standards report is about enforcement, and does not always produce a personal refund.

**Is this the same as a faulty goods claim?**

No. This is about the advertised price. If the item is also faulty, you have separate rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015.
