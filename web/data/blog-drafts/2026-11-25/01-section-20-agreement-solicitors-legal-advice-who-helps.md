## Summary

A **section 20 agreement** under the Children Act 1989 is how a local authority can accommodate a child when a person with parental responsibility agrees. The child then becomes a looked after child. Who helps starts with independent advice before you sign, from services such as Family Rights Group and Child Law Advice, then legal aid and a **section 20 agreement solicitor** if the case may move toward care proceedings. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** attend a meeting with the local authority as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- This page is about section 20 of the **Children Act 1989**. A different section 20 (in the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985) covers consultation before major works on a leasehold building. If your issue is service charges or building works, see [What Is A Section 20 Consultation?](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas/Home%20and%20Housing/Renting/What%20Is%20A%20Section%2020%20Consultation).
- Consent should be informed and voluntary. You can ask for the proposal in writing, and you can ask for time to take advice before you sign.
- A parent with parental responsibility can usually withdraw consent and ask for the child to come home, unless a court order is in place or the local authority starts proceedings.
- Keep copies of every letter, email and agreement. Note dates and the names of social workers.
- Check whether **legal aid** is available before you meet the local authority again.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Under **section 20** of the Children Act 1989, a local authority may provide accommodation for a child if a person with parental responsibility agrees, or in some other cases set out in the Act, for example where no one with parental responsibility is available.
- The child is a looked after child while accommodated in this way. The local authority has duties to plan and review that accommodation.
- Consent should not be obtained under pressure. You can ask to take independent advice first.
- Withdrawal of consent, and what the local authority may do next, is a point on which you should get advice quickly. A court order is a different legal route from a voluntary agreement.

- Keep a dated file of every plan, agreement and email.

## FAQ

**Is a section 20 agreement the same as a care order?**

No. A care order is made by a court. A section 20 arrangement is intended to be voluntary accommodation with agreement. The legal consequences are different, which is why independent advice matters.

**Can I get free legal advice before I sign?**

Often yes. Family advice services and, in many care related cases, legal aid or a duty solicitor can help. Check eligibility on GOV.UK and ask the service you contact.

**What if my issue is leasehold major works, not children?**

That is a different section 20, under landlord and tenant law. Use the leasehold consultation guide linked above, not this page.
