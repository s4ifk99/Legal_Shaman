## Summary

**Buying a partner out of a property** usually means a transfer of equity: one co owner takes over the property and the other comes off the title and, where there is a mortgage, the lender's record. You typically need a valuation, the lender's consent, and a solicitor or licensed conveyancer. If you cannot agree, advice services can explain options, including an application to the court under the Trusts of Land and Appointment of Trustees Act 1996 in some cases. ## Key Information

- There is no automatic right to force a co owner to sell their share to you. Agreement, a deed, or a court order is what changes ownership.
- The lender must usually agree if there is a mortgage. The person who stays will often need to pass an affordability check and may remortgage.
- A valuation, and a clear record of deposits and contributions, will matter. A declaration of trust, if you have one, is important.
- Stamp Duty Land Tax can apply to a transfer of equity in some cases, including where mortgage debt is taken on. Take tax advice if the figures are not small.
- If you cannot agree, get advice early. Court is a last resort.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Joint owners hold the legal title together. The beneficial shares (who is entitled to the value) depend on how the property is held and on any declaration of trust or contributions.
- A transfer of equity changes who is on the legal title. The Land Registry records that change, and a lender must usually consent if the property is mortgaged.
- If co owners cannot agree about sale or shares, the court has powers under the **Trusts of Land and Appointment of Trustees Act 1996**. That is a different path from a voluntary buyout.
- Married couples and civil partners may also have financial remedy options on divorce or dissolution that sit alongside, or instead of, a simple buyout.

## Practical Guidance

- Gather the title, mortgage statements, any declaration of trust, and a record of deposits and payments.
- Speak to the lender about whether a transfer or remortgage is possible, without assuming it will be agreed.
- If you both agree in principle, instruct a solicitor or licensed conveyancer for the transfer of equity.
- If you cannot agree, contact an advice service and ask about mediation or a TOLATA application before you go to court.

## FAQ

**Can I force my partner to sell their share to me?**

Not as of right. You can make an offer, use any option in a declaration of trust, or in some cases apply to the court about the property. The court may order a sale rather than a buyout.

**What about the mortgage?**

The lender's consent is usually required. The person who stays must normally show they can afford the loan on their own, or find a new deal.

**Do I need a solicitor?**

For a transfer of equity, yes, you will normally each need a conveyancer. Advice services can help you understand the options first, especially if you do not yet agree.
