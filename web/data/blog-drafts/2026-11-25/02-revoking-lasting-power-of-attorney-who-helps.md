## Summary

**Revoking a lasting power of attorney** (LPA) is something the donor can do if they have mental capacity. You complete a deed of revocation and send it to the Office of the Public Guardian, and you tell the attorney. Who helps: GOV.UK for the form, the Office of the Public Guardian for registration and concerns, and advice services if capacity or misuse is in dispute. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** complete the deed as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Only the donor can revoke an LPA, and only while they have mental capacity to do so. After the donor dies, the LPA ends automatically.
- Use the official deed of revocation and send it to the Office of the Public Guardian if the LPA is registered. Also tell each attorney, and any organisation that was using the LPA, such as a bank.
- If you are worried that an attorney is misusing an LPA, you can report a concern to the Office of the Public Guardian. That is a different path from the donor choosing to revoke.
- If there is a dispute about capacity, get advice before you act. The Court of Protection deals with some of these disputes.
- Keep copies of the deed, proof of posting, and any acknowledgements.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- A lasting power of attorney is made under the **Mental Capacity Act 2005** and must be registered with the Office of the Public Guardian before it can be used.
- A donor who has capacity can revoke a registered LPA by a deed of revocation and by notifying the Office of the Public Guardian and the attorney.
- An attorney must follow the Mental Capacity Act principles and the terms of the LPA. Concerns about an attorney can be reported to the Office of the Public Guardian.
- The Court of Protection can deal with disputes about capacity, revocation, or an attorney's conduct.

## Practical Guidance

- If you are the donor and you have capacity, complete the official deed of revocation and send it to the **Office of the Public Guardian**. Tell each attorney in writing.
- Tell banks and other organisations that were relying on the LPA.
- If you are worried about misuse, report a concern to the Office of the Public Guardian and gather records of what the attorney has done.
- If capacity or a family dispute is in issue, get advice before you rely on or reject the LPA.

## FAQ

**Can a family member revoke someone else's LPA?**

No. Only the donor can revoke it, and only if they have capacity. If you are worried about an attorney, use the Office of the Public Guardian reporting route, or get advice about the Court of Protection.

**The LPA was never registered. What then?**

An unregistered LPA cannot be used. You should still tell the attorney that you no longer want it to go ahead, and take advice if a registration has been sent in.

**Does the LPA end when the donor dies?**

Yes. The attorney must stop using it. The executor or administrator then deals with the estate.
