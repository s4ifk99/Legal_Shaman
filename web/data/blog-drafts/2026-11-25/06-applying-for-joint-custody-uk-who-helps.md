## Summary

**Applying for joint custody** is a phrase many people still search. In England and Wales the court no longer makes "custody" orders. It makes a **child arrangements order** about who the child lives with and who they spend time with. Shared care can be written into that order. Who helps: try to agree first, then mediation (a MIAM is usually required before court), then Child Law Advice, Cafcass once a case starts, and legal aid if you qualify. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** represent you at a hearing as your solicitor.

## Key Information

- Use the legal term **child arrangements order**, even if you came here searching for joint custody. The form is usually C100.
- The court's first concern is the child's welfare, not a 50/50 split as of right.
- You should usually attend a MIAM (a mediation information meeting) before you apply, unless an exemption applies, for example in some cases involving domestic abuse.
- Parental responsibility is separate from the "lives with" and "spends time with" arrangements.
- If you are asking about one parent having the child live with them only, see [Applying for sole custody in the UK: who helps](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas/Family%20and%20Relationships/Children/Applying%20for%20sole%20custody%20in%20the%20UK%3A%20who%20helps).

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- "Custody" and "joint custody" are no longer the orders the court makes. Under the **Children Act 1989** the court can make a child arrangements order.
- That order can say that a child lives with both parents, or lives with one and spends time with the other. The label is less important than what the order actually says.
- The child's welfare is the court's paramount consideration. There is no automatic right to equal time.
- Cafcass (or Cafcass Cymru in Wales) may report to the court once an application is issued.

## Practical Guidance

- Try to agree a plan in writing if it is safe to do so. A family mediator can help you talk it through.
- Attend a **MIAM** unless an exemption applies, then consider form **C100** if you still need a court order.
- Check **legal aid** if there is domestic abuse or a child protection issue.
- Once a case is issued, read what **Cafcass** or Cafcass Cymru asks you to do, and meet the deadlines.

## FAQ

**Is joint custody automatic if we both have parental responsibility?**

No. Parental responsibility is about decision making. The living and contact pattern is a separate arrangement, by agreement or by a child arrangements order.

**Do I have to go to court?**

Not if you can agree. Court is for when you cannot agree, or when an order is needed to make the plan clear and enforceable.

**Can I get legal aid?**

Sometimes, for example where there is evidence of domestic abuse or a child protection concern. Check on GOV.UK and ask the advice services you contact.
