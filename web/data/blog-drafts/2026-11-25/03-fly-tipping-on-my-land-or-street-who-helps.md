## Summary

**Fly tipping** is the illegal dumping of waste. If it is on a public road or council land, report it to the local council, which should investigate and arrange clearance. If it is on private land, the occupier or landowner is often responsible for clearance, but you should still report it so the council can investigate. Who helps: the council first, the Environment Agency for large or hazardous loads, and advice services if a neighbour or a fly tipping fine is in dispute. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** clear the waste for you.

## Key Information

- Report fly tipping to your local council through [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/report-flytipping). Give the exact location, photos, and any vehicle details.
- Do not go through the waste yourself looking for names. Some waste is hazardous.
- On private land, ask the council what it will investigate and what clearance sits with you. Keep receipts if you have to clear it.
- If you have been accused of fly tipping, or you have a fine with a deadline, use the existing pages on those situations instead of this one.
- A solicitor is more likely to be useful where waste is repeatedly dumped, the loss is large, or you need to recover clearance costs.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- Fly tipping is an offence under the **Environmental Protection Act 1990**. It is a crime to dump waste illegally.
- Local councils investigate most fly tipping on public land and can issue fixed penalty notices or prosecute.
- The Environment Agency deals with larger, organised or particularly harmful cases.
- On private land, the occupier or landowner may have to arrange clearance, even when they did not dump the waste. Reporting still matters, because the council may be able to investigate the offender.

## Practical Guidance

- Photograph the waste and the location, then report it to your **local council** via GOV.UK. Do not disturb hazardous material.
- Ask the council for a reference number and what happens next.
- For large or hazardous loads, ask whether the **Environment Agency** should also be notified.
- If it is happening now, contact the **police** on 101, or 999 if you are unsafe.
- If a neighbour or a contractor is involved, write to them and keep copies. Take advice before you try to recover costs. Check your **insurer** if you have to pay for clearance on private land.

## FAQ

**The waste is on my private driveway. Will the council clear it?**

Often no. Councils usually clear public land. They may still investigate. Ask your council what it will do, and keep records if you pay for clearance.

**I have a vehicle registration from the dumping. What should I do?**

Include it in the council report. Do not confront the driver yourself.

**I received a fly tipping fine I want to challenge.**

That is a different problem. Use the who helps page on fly tipping fines linked above.
