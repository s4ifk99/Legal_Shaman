## Summary

If a **gift card** is declined in a shop that is still trading, check the terms, the expiry date, and whether the card was already spent. The retailer should honour a valid card. If the shop has closed or the company has gone into insolvency, a gift card is often treated as an unsecured claim, which may be worth little. If the card was bought with a credit card, **Section 75** or chargeback may still help. The free doors below come from Legal Shaman Matching Help. They will **not** claim in the insolvency for you.

## Key Information

- Photograph the card, the till receipt, and any terms on the sleeve or website. Note the date, the shop, and what the assistant said.
- A retailer that is still trading should accept a valid, in date card according to its terms. Write to customer services first.
- If the company has gone into administration or liquidation, contact the insolvency practitioner named in the notice. Ask whether gift cards will receive anything. Do not assume a full refund.
- If you (or the buyer) paid by credit card and the card cost was between £100 and £30,000, ask the card issuer about **Section 75**. Chargeback may also be available, with shorter time limits.
- A gift card you received as a present is harder to claim on if you do not have the original payment details. Ask the buyer to contact their card issuer.

## What the law says in England and Wales

This section explains the general law. It is not advice on your situation.

- A gift card is usually a contract with the issuer or retailer. Terms on expiry, which shops accept the card, and balances should be clear. Unfair terms can be challenged.
- If the retailer is still trading and refuses a valid card, that can be a breach of contract. A complaint, then the relevant ombudsman or ADR scheme if there is one, may be open to you.
- If the company is insolvent, gift card holders are usually unsecured creditors. There is no general public fund that pays out unused gift cards.
- **Section 75** of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 can make a credit card issuer jointly liable for a breach of contract or misrepresentation on qualifying purchases. Chargeback is a card scheme process, not a statute, and time limits are tight.

## Practical Guidance

- Write to the retailer or card issuer with the card number, the balance, and what happened at the till, and ask them to honour it or refund it.
- If the shop has closed, find the insolvency notice and contact the appointed insolvency practitioner.
- If the card was bought on a credit or debit card, ask the **card issuer** about **Section 75** or chargeback without delay.
- If the bank refuses, complain to the bank first, then the **Financial Ombudsman Service**.

## FAQ

**The shop is still open but the till declines my card.**

Ask for the reason in writing, check the terms and expiry, and complain to customer services. If the card is valid, they should honour it or explain the legal basis for the refusal.

**The retailer has gone bust. Is my gift card worthless?**

Often it has little value in the insolvency. It is still worth notifying the insolvency practitioner, and asking the person who paid about Section 75 or chargeback.

**I was given the card as a present. Can I use Section 75?**

Section 75 is for the person who paid on the credit card. Ask the buyer to contact their issuer and to share the receipt with you.
