## Summary

If you paid a seller for physical **souvenir tickets** and they arrived **late and damaged**, who helps is that seller, in writing, under the **Consumer Rights Act 2015**. Damaged goods are a satisfactory quality problem. Very late delivery can be a separate point if a date was agreed or the delay mattered. A refusal email that says they “cannot refund or replace” is the start of the complaint, not the end of it.

In **Scotland**, advice is from Citizens Advice Scotland or Advice Direct Scotland, not the England consumer script alone. If you still need a court, the route for a modest money claim is **simple procedure** in the sheriff court, generally for £5,000 or less. Money Claim Online is the England and Wales system. Do not use it for a Scottish claim.

The 14 day cooling off period for changing your mind about an online order is a different right. It will often have expired if you bought many months ago. Lead with the damage and the lateness, not with “I changed my mind”.

## Key Information

- Photograph the damage, keep the packaging, and save the order page that described the souvenir and any delivery date.
- Your contract is with the seller who took the money. If a marketplace, a promoter and a printer are all in the emails, the first letter still goes to the trader you paid.
- Inside 30 days of receiving faulty goods, a short term rejection can be available. If the items sat unopened for longer, you may be in repair, replace, or price reduction territory. Say when they arrived and when you found the damage.
- A credit card payment over £100 can be put to the card issuer as **Section 75**, alongside the seller complaint. A debit card is usually **chargeback**, which has scheme time limits, so do not wait months after the seller’s refusal.
- Scotland’s simple procedure can include a money claim of £5,000 or less. It is not the first step. The Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service tells you to try to settle, then use Civil Online.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

In Scotland, an adviser will help you tell a fault claim from a change of mind, and will point you to a written complaint before court. The England template [letter to complain about faulty goods](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/template-letters/letters/consumer-template-letters/letter-to-complain-about-faulty-goods/) is a useful shape. Use the Scotland version of the advice where the site offers it, and the consumer helpline **0808 223 1133** still routes Scotland calls. They will not email the ticket company or issue the sheriff court claim.

## What the card issuer would actually do here

The issuer can consider **Section 75** or **chargeback** when the seller has been asked and has refused, and you send the order, the photos and the refusal. They will not inspect the tickets. They will not usually pay out while you have never written to the seller. If they refuse a Section 75 claim, the next free door is the [Financial Ombudsman Service](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/) after the issuer’s final response.

## Practical Guidance

- **Seller** complaints team: photos, order number, the date they arrived, and what you want (replacement or refund of the souvenir price). Give 14 days.
- **Citizens Advice** if the reply is a flat “no refunds on souvenirs”.
- **Card issuer** as soon as you have that refusal, especially on a debit card where chargeback clocks are shorter.
- **Simple procedure** only after the letter and the card route. Read [how to make a claim](https://www.scotcourts.gov.uk/taking-action/simple-procedure/how-to-make-a-claim-with-simple-procedure/) and use Civil Online. The usual court is where the seller has a place of business, which may not be your local town if you bought from a national company. Ask the sheriff clerk if you are unsure.

## FAQ

**The concert already happened. Is it too late?**
The event ticket and the paid for souvenir are not the same product. If the souvenir itself arrived damaged, the goods complaint can still be made. Do it in writing now. Card scheme clocks may already be tight.

**They say souvenirs are not refundable.**
A policy line does not cancel statutory rights where goods are not of satisfactory quality. Use the faulty goods letter, not the cooling off letter, unless you are still inside 14 days of delivery and you simply do not want them.

**I am in England, not Scotland.**
The seller complaint and the card doors are the same idea. The court brand changes: Civil Money Claims in England and Wales, simple procedure in Scotland.
