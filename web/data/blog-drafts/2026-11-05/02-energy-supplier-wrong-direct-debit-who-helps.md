## Summary

If an **energy supplier** collects the **wrong direct debit** (the wrong amount, or weekly when you agreed monthly), who helps first is the supplier complaints team. Ask them to correct the instruction, refund what should not have been taken, and confirm the next collection in writing. Where there was an error in the payment, the **Direct Debit Guarantee** is a bank door: your bank or building society should give a full and immediate refund of the amount paid. That refund does not wipe a genuine energy bill. The supplier can still bill you for energy you used.

**Citizens Advice** can explain the complaint order and the difference between a payment error and a higher bill you simply disagree with. **Ofgem** does not decide your individual refund. The **Energy Ombudsman** can, after you have given the supplier eight weeks or received a deadlock letter.

## Key Information

- Keep the mandate, the emails about the amount, and the bank statement lines that show the extra collections.
- The Direct Debit Guarantee, as published by the scheme, says the organisation should tell you **10 working days** before a change to the amount, date or frequency, unless you agreed something else. If there is an error by the organisation or by the bank, you are entitled to a full and immediate refund from the bank of the amount paid.
- A refund under the guarantee can be reclaimed later if you were not entitled to it. Budget for the supplier asking for the correct monthly amount.
- If the money leaving the account has left you unable to pay rent, council tax or food, say that in the complaint and ask about the supplier Priority Services Register. That is a support flag, not a write off.
- Cancelling the Direct Debit at the bank stops future collections. It does not erase charges already built up, and some tariffs cost more if you stop paying that way.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

The consumer helpline will help you separate three things people mix up: a collection error, a price rise, and a back bill. They can explain the supplier complaint, the bank guarantee, and when the **Energy Ombudsman** opens. They will not log into your energy account, reverse a payment, or negotiate a payment plan for you. Helpline **0808 223 1133**.

## What the Energy Ombudsman would actually do here

The ombudsman looks at supplier and some network disputes after eight weeks, or sooner with a deadlock letter. They can tell a supplier to refund, correct an account, apologise, or pay a small amount for the trouble. They will not take the case on day one, and they do not replace Ofgem’s rule making. Ofgem supervises firms. It does not umpire your bill. Start here only after the supplier stage: [Energy Ombudsman](https://www.energyombudsman.org/we-may-be-able-to-help-resolve-your-energy-dispute). Phone **0330 440 1624**.

## What the bank would actually do here

If you tell the bank there was an error in the Direct Debit, the guarantee says you get a full and immediate refund of that payment from the bank. The bank is not deciding whether your energy tariff is fair. If the bank refuses, complain to the bank and then the [Financial Ombudsman Service](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/). The scheme itself explains the limit: the guarantee covers errors in setup or collection, not every dispute about the underlying bill. Read [your rights and safeguards](https://www.directdebit.co.uk/help/your-rights-and-safeguards).

## Practical Guidance

- **Supplier** complaints team, in writing: what was agreed, what was taken, the dates, and what you want (refund of the extra, a corrected monthly figure, written notice before the next change).
- **Bank** under the **Direct Debit Guarantee** if the collection itself was an error. Say that phrase. Ask for the refund of the wrong payment.
- **Citizens Advice** if you are unsure which of those two doors fits, or you need a breathing space conversation because the extra debit caused hardship.
- **Energy Ombudsman** at eight weeks or on a deadlock letter. Do not wait past the 12 month window that runs from the supplier final decision. The existing wiki page [Complain to the Energy Ombudsman](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas%2FRights%20and%20Discrimination%2FHuman%20Rights%20and%20Public%20Law%2FComplain%20to%20the%20Energy%20Ombudsman) covers that time limit.
- **Ofgem** only to report a firm that is breaking licence rules, not to get your money back this week.

## FAQ

**Can I get the extra money back this week?**
Ask the supplier and, where the collection was an error, ask the bank under the **Direct Debit Guarantee**. Neither door means the energy was free.

**The supplier set it to weekly instead of monthly. Is that a price rise?**
Not by itself. It is a change to frequency and amount. That is a complaint about the instruction, and it may also be a guarantee point if you were not told properly. A higher unit rate is a different page: [Your direct debit payments have increased](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas%2FMoney%2C%20Benefits%20and%20Debt%2FTax%20and%20Bills%2FYour%20direct%20debit%20payments%20have%20increased).

**Should I cancel the Direct Debit?**
You can. Do it with the bank, and tell the supplier. Then agree how the correct bill will be paid so the account does not fall into arrears while you argue about the extra.
