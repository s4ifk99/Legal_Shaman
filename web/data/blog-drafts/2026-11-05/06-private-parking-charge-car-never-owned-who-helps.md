## Summary

If a **private parking** company says you are the keeper of a **car you have never owned**, and the alleged parking was in a place you have never been, who helps is the operator first. Reply in writing that you are not the keeper, attach the DVLA letter if you have one, and ask them to cancel the notice. Do not pay to “make it go away” while that point is unanswered. Paying can be treated as closing the dispute.

This is not the same problem as a **parking ticket** where you were there but the **wrong registration** was typed into an app. That sibling page is for your own car. This page is for a vehicle that is not yours at all.

**Citizens Advice** will explain the difference between a private Parking Charge Notice and a council penalty, and when to appeal. **POPLA** (British Parking Association members) or the IAS (International Parking Community members) only opens after the operator rejects your appeal and sends a code. They will not look at a notice you have not challenged with the operator first.

## Key Information

- The notice should name the operator, the vehicle registration, the place and the time. If the registration is not a car you have ever owned or kept, say that plainly.
- Keeper liability on private land, where it applies, comes from Schedule 4 of the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012. POPLA’s own material says the keeper is presumed to be the registered keeper unless the contrary is proved. A DVLA letter that you are not the keeper is the proof you send. You do not have to name a driver of a car that is not yours.
- Check the operator’s trade body on the notice or on the [BPA](https://www.britishparking.co.uk/) or IPC member lists before you pick POPLA or IAS. The wrong appeal service will bounce you.
- Deadlines on the notice are real. A late appeal can push you toward a court claim. If a County Court claim form arrives, file a defence. Ignoring a claim is how a default judgment appears. Citizens Advice and the court form both say this. A parking blog does not replace the defence deadline printed on the claim.
- Do not send the operator extra personal data they do not need. The DVLA letter and a short denial are enough to start.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

Advisers explain which notice you hold and whether an appeal is the right tool. Their page on [when to appeal a parking ticket](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/law-and-courts/parking-tickets/when-to-appeal-a-parking-ticket/) covers private land and council land. The page about [someone else driving your car](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/law-and-courts/parking-tickets/appealing-a-parking-ticket-when-someone-else-was-driving/) is a different situation: that is your car and another driver. Use it only if the registration really is yours. Advisers will not email the parking firm or guarantee the charge will be cancelled.

## What POPLA would actually do here

POPLA assesses appeals for BPA operators after the operator rejects you and issues a 10 digit verification code. You send the evidence with the appeal. They will not add documents later, and they will not reopen a decision you dislike. If you win, the operator cancels the charge. If you lose, you can still refuse to pay and defend a later claim, but you should read the decision first. Start at [how to appeal](https://www.popla.co.uk/appeal-guide). IAS is the IPC scheme. It is not POPLA. Use the scheme named on the rejection.

## Practical Guidance

- **Operator**, before the date on the notice: “I am not the registered keeper of this vehicle. I have never owned it or kept it. Please cancel.” Attach the DVLA letter. Keep proof of sending.
- **DVLA** if you do not yet have written confirmation. Ask for a record you can show the operator. Do not rely on a phone call alone.
- **Citizens Advice** if the operator ignores the DVLA letter or tells you to name a driver anyway.
- **POPLA or IAS** only after a rejection and only on the scheme that matches the operator, inside the code deadline (often 28 days from the rejection for POPLA).
- **Court papers**: acknowledge and defend. Do not throw them away because you know the car is not yours. The judge does not know that until you say it on the form.

## FAQ

**I typed my plate wrong at a car park I did use. Is this the page?**
No. Use [Parking ticket wrong registration: who helps?](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas/Driving%20and%20Parking/Parking%20and%20PCNs/Parking%20ticket%20wrong%20registration%3A%20who%20helps). That one needs your payment receipt and the correct plate. This page is for a car that was never yours.

**Should I phone them and give my full address history?**
No. Reply on the appeal route with the keeper point and the DVLA letter. Extra biography does not help.

**What if they keep writing after I prove it?**
Keep every letter. Send one chase that attaches the proof again. If a claim form comes, defend it. If the contact becomes harassment rather than a genuine chase of a disputed charge, Citizens Advice can explain the next complaint. Do not ignore a court form either way.
