## Summary

If a private **landlord** in England, after a long tenancy, wants to keep the **deposit** for worn carpets and for the landlord’s own time writing letters, who helps is the tenancy **deposit scheme** that holds the money. The deposit is your money unless the landlord proves a loss. **Fair wear and tear** is not a deduction. Schemes look at how long you lived there and how many people lived there. A family home after several years is expected to show more wear than a six month let.

**Shelter** and **Citizens Advice** will explain what a landlord can and cannot keep, and how to raise the scheme dispute. They will not argue the adjudication for you or pay the deposit out. If the deposit was never protected, the scheme door may not exist and a court claim is the path Shelter describes, including a possible compensation claim for the protection failure. That is a different page from a protected deposit you simply disagree with.

## Key Information

- Ask, in writing, which scheme protects the deposit and for the breakdown: each head of loss, with invoices or quotes. “My time” is not an invoice for a repair.
- Fair wear and tear, in Shelter’s words, means changes from normal living: scuffs, faded fabric, worn or frayed flooring. Landlords cannot keep deposit money for that.
- Even where there is damage beyond wear, the landlord cannot charge new for old if a clean or a repair would do, and they cannot charge the full price of a new carpet when the old one was already part way through its life. The amount has to reflect their loss, not a better home than they started with.
- Scheme adjudicators start from the position that the deposit is yours. The landlord has to prove the deduction with the inventory, photos and quotes. Your check in photos and the check out report matter. If you did not get a proper inventory, say so.
- England and Wales schemes you will usually see are the Deposit Protection Service (DPS), the Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS) and mydeposits. Scotland and Northern Ireland use other schemes. Use the one named on your prescribed information, not a scheme you have only heard of.
- A dispute is free. Court is not the first step when the scheme can decide and the landlord will accept the result. If they will not use the scheme, Shelter’s route is a court claim for the deposit.

## What Shelter would actually do here

Shelter’s housing advice sets out [what a landlord can deduct](https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/tenancy_deposits/what_can_your_landlord_deduct_from_your_deposit) and [how to dispute unfair deductions](https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/tenancy_deposits/how_to_get_your_deposit_back/dispute_unfair_deductions). They explain the evidence and the scheme phone numbers. They will not attend the property, value the carpet, or email your landlord as your solicitor. If you are at risk of homelessness or the dispute is tied to an illegal eviction, use Shelter’s emergency routes. A deposit row on its own is the scheme path.

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

An adviser can help you write the email that refuses the time charge and the wear charge, and can explain the unprotected deposit penalty in outline. They will not transfer the money. The wiki pages [Getting your tenancy deposit back](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas/Home%20and%20Housing/Renting/Getting%20your%20tenancy%20deposit%20back) and [Getting your tenancy deposit back if you rent privately](https://www.legalshaman.com/ask-the-shaman/wiki/Areas/Home%20and%20Housing/Renting/Getting%20your%20tenancy%20deposit%20back%20if%20you%20rent%20privately) are the wider maps. This page is only the unfair end of tenancy bill.

## Practical Guidance

- **Landlord or agent**, once, in writing: you do not agree, you want the scheme details, and you want the undisputed balance paid now. Do not trade a long argument for a delay in releasing the part nobody disputes.
- **Deposit scheme** dispute as soon as negotiation stalls. Shelter lists DPS **0330 303 0030**, TDS **0300 037 1000**, mydeposits **0333 321 9401**. Use the scheme website so you hit the right deadline. Deadlines are often short once the landlord asks the scheme to keep the money.
- **Shelter** or **Citizens Advice** before you agree a “goodwill” cut that still pays the landlord for their time.
- **Court** if the deposit was not protected, or the landlord refuses adjudication. There is a fee. Help with fees may be available if you are on a low income.

## FAQ

**Can a landlord charge for the hours they spent on the checkout?**
Scheme rules are aimed at the landlord’s actual loss: unpaid rent, missing items, damage beyond wear. Preparing the dispute is each side’s own cost. Ask the scheme to see a quote for a real loss, not a day rate for the landlord’s time.

**The carpets were already old. Do I still pay to replace them?**
Not for ordinary wear after a long tenancy. Shelter’s example is a budget carpet that is threadbare after years of ordinary use. That is not a tenant charge. Stains, burns or holes can be a contribution, and even then not always the full new price.

**The agent took the deposit and the landlord says ask the agent.**
The landlord remains responsible for returning a deposit that should come back to you. Say that in writing to both. Shelter makes the same point.
