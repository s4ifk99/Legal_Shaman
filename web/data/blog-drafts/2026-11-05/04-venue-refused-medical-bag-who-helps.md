## Summary

If a stadium, arena or other **venue** stops you bringing a small **medical bag** that you need because of a disability, even though their own access policy allows medically necessary items after a check, who helps is the organiser in writing first. The **Equality Act 2010** requires service providers to make **reasonable adjustments** where a practice puts a disabled person at a substantial disadvantage. A bag search can be a lawful security step. A blanket refusal that ignores the stated medical exception is the thing to complain about.

The **Equality Advisory Support Service (EASS)** explains how the Act applies and can help you try to sort it out without a claim. **Citizens Advice** can help you write the request. Neither will represent you in the county court, and neither will tell you that this incident will win.

This is about access to a service, not about discrimination at work. Employment doors such as ACAS are the wrong queue.

## Key Information

- Write while the detail is fresh: date, gate, what you carry and why, what the published policy said, what staff said, and whether you missed part of the event or had to abandon medication at the gate.
- A service provider’s duty to adjust is anticipatory. They should have thought about disabled customers before you arrived. That still does not mean every requested change is reasonable. Cost, security, and whether the change would work all matter. EASS and Citizens Advice explain that test. They do not apply it to your facts as a judgment.
- Ask for a practical outcome: an apology, a note on your booking, a clear gate procedure next time, and any refund of the part of the day you could not use. Do not lead with a damages figure if what you want is the adjustment.
- The Equality and Human Rights Commission publishes the law. It does not usually take individual complaints. The helpline that does is EASS.
- Act promptly if you may need a court claim later. Time limits in discrimination cases are short. Ask EASS what limit applies to a service claim before you assume you have months to spare.

## What the Equality Advisory Support Service would actually do here

EASS can explain the **Equality Act 2010**, how **reasonable adjustments** work for a service, and ways to resolve the problem informally. In some cases they write to the service provider to start that conversation, or point you to conciliation. They do not give legal advice, they do not say how strong the case is, and they stop helping with the case once you issue a court claim. Helpline **0808 800 0082**, Monday to Friday 9am to 7pm, Saturday 10am to 2pm. Contact form on [equalityadvisoryservice.com](https://www.equalityadvisoryservice.com/). GOV.UK lists the same service at [Equality Advisory Support Service](https://www.gov.uk/equality-advisory-support-service).

## What Citizens Advice would actually do here

An adviser can help you turn the night into a short written request: how you are disabled under the Act, what practice caused the problem (the bag rule as it was applied), and what adjustment you need next time. Use their page on [asking for reasonable adjustments](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/law-and-courts/discrimination/check-what-type-of-discrimination-youve-experienced/asking-for-reasonable-adjustments/). They will not contact the club or the promoter for you as a lawyer would, and they will not start the claim.

## Practical Guidance

- **Venue or promoter** complaints address, in writing, within a few days. Quote their published medical exception if you still have it (screenshot the page).
- **EASS** if you want help understanding the Act or an informal approach to the organiser.
- **Citizens Advice** if you need help structuring the letter or you also have a consumer refund question about the ticket.
- **Court** only after advice on the time limit, and only if the outcome you need cannot be got by a letter. County court claims for discrimination are formal, can carry cost risk, and are not a small claims refund for a faulty kettle.

## FAQ

**They searched other bags. Is that enough?**
A search can be a reasonable security step. The complaint is narrower: you needed a **medical bag**, their policy said medically necessary items could come in after inspection, and staff still refused. Put both facts in the letter.

**Is this an employment case?**
No. ACAS and employment tribunals are for work. A ticketed event is a service. Use EASS and the venue complaint.

**Can I get compensation?**
Sometimes a court can award it. The first useful outcomes are usually a proper adjustment and a refund of what you could not use. Ask EASS before you treat compensation as the plan.
