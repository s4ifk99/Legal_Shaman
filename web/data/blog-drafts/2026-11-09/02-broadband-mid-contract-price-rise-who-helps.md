## Summary

If your **broadband** raised the price mid contract and will not let you leave cleanly, who helps starts with a written complaint to the provider, then the free doors Matching Help returns (often Ofcom and Citizens Advice). Those doors will **not** rewrite your contract or waive every exit fee for you.

## Key Information

- Keep the original contract, price rise letter or email, and any exit fee quote.
- Complain to the provider first and get a deadlock or eight week wait before many ADR schemes open.
- Price rise mid term and mid contract exit rights depend on the wording you were sold. Read the notice dates carefully.

## What Financial Ombudsman Service would actually do here

Look at whether a bank, insurer, or other financial firm handled a complaint fairly.

What they will **not** do: Will not take a case until you complained to the firm, and will not cover every non financial dispute.

## What Citizens Advice Adviceline (England) would actually do here

Explain your rights, point to templates, and route you to local or specialist help.

What they will **not** do: Will not chase the other party or represent you in court.

## What Ofcom would actually do here

Explain how telecoms complaints and ADR work after you complain to the provider.

What they will **not** do: Will not rewrite your broadband contract or decide your exit fee for you.

## Practical Guidance

- Write to the **broadband provider** complaints team asking to leave without an unfair exit fee or to reverse the rise.
- Keep proof of sending and the complaint reference.
- **Financial Ombudsman Service**: [Open](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/).
- **Citizens Advice Adviceline (England)**: [Open](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/contact-us/contact-us/advice-line/).
- **Ofcom**: [Open](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/about-ofcom/what-we-do/contact-us).
